\begin{abstract}
We study whether bounded-memory heavy-hitter sketches can improve masked language model pretraining under BabyLM Strict-Small data constraints. Our method, Heavy-Hitter Masking (HHM), keeps the dataset, tokenizer, architecture, optimizer, training schedule, and total mask rate fixed, but reallocates part of the masked-token budget toward repeated local entity mentions and tokens that recently produced high prediction loss. Across three matched 10k-step local training runs, HHM does not improve validation loss over random masking: the random baseline reaches $3.0023 \pm 0.1576$, while HHM reaches $3.0321 \pm 0.1469$. Mask diagnostics show that the intervention is partially diluted by fallback random masking, with observed HHM masks split 15.4\% entity, 18.9\% error, and 65.6\% random. Official BabyLM strict zero-shot evaluation gives a mixed counterpoint: HHM is slightly higher than the random baseline on the available BLiMP, Supplement, Entity Tracking, COMPS, and reading metrics, but the gains are small and sometimes high-variance. Follow-up pilots show that raw loss-guided CMS-Morph and Entity-Accuracy-Morph do not improve validation loss, while a simpler correctness-smoothed Accuracy-Morph variant improves matched validation loss in two seeds, with mean paired change $-0.0155 \pm 0.0051$, and raises Entity Tracking by $+5.51 \pm 3.27$. We therefore present HHM as a controlled mixed result and Accuracy-Morph as the most promising follow-up.
\end{abstract}
