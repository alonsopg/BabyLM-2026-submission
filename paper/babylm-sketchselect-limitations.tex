\section{Limitations}

The broad idea of adaptive or selective masking has substantial prior work. This paper does not claim to introduce adaptive masking, prediction-guided masking, or selective masking as new paradigms. Its contribution is the narrower controlled comparison of several online hard-to-predict masking signals under the same small-language-model pretraining setup.

The main HHM result does not improve validation loss. The available official BabyLM diagnostic gains are small, descriptive, and not statistically significant in the three-seed paired tests. They should be treated as suggestive diagnostics rather than as evidence of a robust downstream improvement.

Accuracy-Morph is the most promising follow-up, but it has only two matched seeds. It should not be presented as a confirmed method. The safe conclusion is that smoothed prediction correctness deserves multi-seed follow-up, not that hard-to-predict word masking has been shown to improve small language model pretraining.

The term hard-to-predict is operationalized by model prediction behavior during MLM pretraining, not by human judgments of linguistic difficulty or external linguistic annotation. A token can be hard for many reasons, including rarity, ambiguity, tokenization, or insufficient context. This is one reason raw high-loss signals can be noisy.

The current implementation uses heuristic token-level entity extraction rather than a full syntactic or coreference system. Error heavy hitters are keyed by target token string in the first implementation, so they do not distinguish different contexts for the same token. The local BabyLM evaluation bundle did not include EWoK, so we do not report EWoK and cannot assess that part of the full BabyLM suite here. The component ablations reported here are short 20-step checks and should not be read as final ablation evidence.

The paper also does not attempt to match the strongest BabyLM systems. We do not use hybrid causal-masked objectives, architecture changes, optimizer changes, curated corpora, external lexical resources, downstream task labels, or auxiliary representation modules. That restriction is scientifically useful for attribution, but it limits expected absolute performance and means the conclusions depend on this specific small LM architecture and 10k-step training budget. A stronger final system would likely combine the best controlled masking signal here, Accuracy-Morph, with a stronger BabyLM backbone; that experiment would answer a different question from the one isolated in this paper.
