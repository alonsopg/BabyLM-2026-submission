\begin{abstract}
This paper asks whether small language models can pretrain more effectively when part of the masked-language-modeling budget is allocated to words that are hard for the model to predict, rather than selected uniformly at random. We study this question under BabyLM Strict-Small constraints by keeping the dataset, tokenizer, architecture, optimizer, schedule, training length, and total 15\% mask rate fixed, changing only which token positions are selected for prediction. We compare several online signals for identifying hard-to-predict words, including repeated local token heavy hitters, recent high-loss tokens, Count-Min Sketch signals over token and character-trigram difficulty, and smoothed prediction-correctness signals. In a three-seed comparison, Heavy-Hitter Masking does not improve validation loss over random masking, although it produces small gains on the available official BabyLM diagnostics. Follow-up experiments show that raw-loss-based CMS-Morph and Entity-Accuracy-Morph do not improve validation loss, while Accuracy-Morph, which uses smoothed prediction correctness, improves matched validation loss in two seeds and gives the clearest Entity Tracking gains. We present the results as a controlled diagnostic study rather than as a settled positive result: naive hard-token masking is insufficient, raw loss is a noisy difficulty signal, and correctness-smoothed masking is the most promising signal tested so far.
\end{abstract}
