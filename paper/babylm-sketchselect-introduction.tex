\section{Introduction}

The BabyLM challenge asks whether language models can learn robust linguistic generalizations from developmentally plausible data budgets rather than from web-scale corpora \citep{warstadt2023babylm,hu2024babylm,choshen2026babylm}. Under the Strict-Small track, models are trained on at most 10M words, making each prediction target valuable. Recent successful BabyLM systems often improve efficiency by changing the model architecture, training objective, optimizer, or data pipeline \citep{charpentier2024gptbert,yu2024antlm,haller2025sample,kosmopoulou2025masked}. This paper instead asks how far a much smaller intervention can go.

We propose \textsc{Heavy-Hitter Masking} (HHM), a masking-only intervention for masked language model pretraining. Rather than changing the corpus or model, HHM changes which token positions are selected for prediction. The total mask rate remains fixed at 15\%, matching the random masking baseline. This places the work in the line of adaptive MLM masking \citep{lee2022efficient,yang2023learning,zhong2023self,edman2025mask}, but with a stricter ablation-style control: no external knowledge graph, no auxiliary loss, no subtoken embedding module, no architecture change, and no additional data.

The research question is: can bounded-memory heavy-hitter sketches improve small-data language model pretraining by reallocating a fixed masked-language-modeling budget toward repeated discourse entities and model-specific hard examples? HHM uses two compact signals: local entity heavy hitters, intended to emphasize recurring discourse participants, and online error heavy hitters, intended to revisit tokens the current model has recently struggled to predict.

The contribution is a controlled workshop-style evaluation of a cheap, inspectable masking policy. In the completed local training comparison, HHM is a small negative result on MLM validation loss: it reallocates the mask budget as intended, but its mean validation loss is slightly worse than random masking after 10k steps. This still clarifies the design space. The diagnostic logs show that entity and error heavy hitters account for only about one third of realized masks, with the rest filled by random fallback, suggesting that the current heuristic mixture may be too diluted to substantially improve pretraining loss. Official BabyLM evaluation of the same checkpoints gives a more nuanced outcome: HHM is slightly higher on the available official zero-shot metrics, so the method may affect linguistic diagnostics even when it does not improve the local training objective.

We treat this negative result as part of the contribution rather than hiding it behind a single best run. The experiments separate three questions that are often bundled together in stronger systems: whether adaptive masking can be implemented without changing the training budget, whether raw model loss is a useful online difficulty signal, and whether subtoken difficulty can make the signal less brittle. This leads to the Accuracy-Morph follow-up, which replaces raw loss with smoothed top-1 correctness and adds character-trigram difficulty sketches while preserving the same 15\% mask rate.

After the main comparison, we also tested masking-only follow-ups: CMS-Morph, Accuracy-Morph, and Entity-Accuracy-Morph. These pilots sharpen the current recommendation. Raw high-loss CMS sketches and entity-bonus decay do not improve validation loss, but the simpler Accuracy-Morph variant, which scores candidates by smoothed prediction correctness, improves matched validation loss in two seeds and produces the clearest Entity Tracking gains. We therefore frame HHM as a mixed controlled result and Accuracy-Morph as the most plausible next experiment rather than as a settled conclusion.
