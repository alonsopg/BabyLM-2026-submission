\section{Masking Analysis}

We analyze whether HHM changes the distribution of prediction targets while preserving the same total number of masked tokens as the baseline. The implementation logs total masked tokens, entity-selected masks, error-selected masks, and fallback random masks for every training step.

\paragraph{Fairness Checks.}
Unit tests verify that random masking and HHM mask the same number of valid positions, that padding and special tokens are never masked, that entity positions correspond to repeated local strings, that the error sketch is inactive during warmup, and that masking is deterministic under a fixed seed.

\paragraph{Observed Allocation.}
Across the three completed 10k-step HHM runs, the collator masks the same average number of tokens per step as the random baseline: 1215.8 valid tokens. The source of those HHM masks is 15.4\% entity-selected positions, 18.9\% error-selected positions, and 65.6\% fallback random positions. This verifies that HHM changes the target distribution without increasing the total prediction budget, but it also shows that the intended 25/25/50 entity-error-random mixture is not fully realized.

\paragraph{Interpretation.}
The high fallback rate gives a concrete explanation for the weak validation-loss result. Entity and error sketches are active, but the realized intervention is closer to a mostly random masking policy with a moderate heavy-hitter bias than to an evenly balanced three-source mixture. The official evaluation suggests that this moderate shift may still affect measured linguistic behavior: HHM is slightly higher than the random baseline on the available official zero-shot tasks, including Entity Tracking and COMPS. However, the gains are small and often well within seed variation, so they should be treated as a diagnostic signal rather than a reliable win.

The pilot diagnostics point to a narrower lesson. Increasing adaptivity is not automatically helpful: CMS-Morph assigns a larger final adaptive share than Accuracy-Morph but has worse validation loss, while Entity-Accuracy-Morph decays to a very small final adaptive share and also loses the Accuracy-Morph advantage. The useful signal appears to be the scoring rule, not simply the amount of non-random masking. In the current runs, smoothed prediction correctness is a cleaner adaptive signal than raw loss or repeated-token bonuses.
